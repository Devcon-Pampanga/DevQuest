## Design Context

### Users

**Who they are:** Filipino student volunteers (ages ~16–22) in the DEVCON Kids program. Two roles: volunteers (default) and coordinators (elevated privileges). Students are tech-curious but not necessarily professional developers yet — this is often their first structured career-building experience.

**Their context when using the app:** Coming off an event, checking their XP after attending a Code Camp, submitting a quest for coordinator approval, or reviewing their portfolio before sharing it with a recruiter. Often on mobile, frequently in between school and volunteer commitments.

**The job to be done:** Track and verify volunteer work as meaningful career milestones. Transform "I helped run an event" into "I earned Facilitator XP and completed the Code Camp milestone" — something exportable, shareable, and credible.

### Brand Personality

**Voice:** Bold · Playful · Energetic

**Tone:** Celebratory and forward-moving. DevQuest should feel like a coach who's genuinely excited about your progress — not a corporate HR system logging your hours.

**Emotional goal:** A volunteer who opens their dashboard should feel **proud of their progress.** The UI constantly reflects how far they've come — visible milestones, XP history, tier badges front and center. Progress is never invisible. Every interaction should reinforce that their effort is real, tracked, and valued.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Dark, colorful, gamified — but professionally credible. Think career tool that grew up inside a game engine. The dark base (#0a0a0f) is the stage; team colors and accent purples are the lights. Never flat, never monochromatic.

**Theme:** Dark-only. No light mode.

**Primary accent:** Purple (#7C3AED primary, #A855F7 highlight) — used on all primary actions, focus rings, CTAs.

**Team colors are first-class citizens:** Lead Learners yellow (#F5C518), P&C orange (#F97316), Community cyan (#06B6D4), Creatives purple (#9333EA), Sustainability green (#22C55E). Use aggressively — card borders, progress bars, stat numbers, section headers, badge rings.

**Anti-reference:** Generic SaaS / corporate. DevQuest must not look like Jira, Linear, Notion, or Google Workspace. No cold blues, no sterile white space, no enterprise-dashboard energy. If it could belong to a B2B productivity startup, it's wrong.

**Reference feel:** Closer to a polished indie game dashboard or a premium gamified learning platform — where every element has personality and the interface itself communicates "you're making real progress." Think RPG character sheet meets career portfolio, not admin panel.

**Accessibility:** Standard contrast, keyboard nav, and semantic HTML. No formal WCAG compliance requirement at this stage.

### Design Principles

1. **Progress is always visible.** XP, tier, quest completion, milestone count — never bury these. A volunteer should feel their effort reflected back to them on every page they visit.

2. **Team identity shapes the interface.** Every volunteer belongs to teams. Their team colors should appear on their content — it's not just decoration, it's identity. Never render all cards the same neutral color when team context is available.

3. **Bold over subtle.** When in doubt, go bolder. Stronger color, heavier type, larger numbers. DevQuest's target users respond to energy and confidence — not minimalist restraint. Quiet designs read as low-effort here.

4. **Gamified but credible.** XP numbers, tier badges, and quest completions are real professional milestones. Treat them with the visual weight of achievements, not novelties. The gamification should feel earned, not trivial.

5. **No corporate sterility.** Every component should have at least one moment of personality — a team color, a glowing border, a bold stat. If a section looks like it belongs in a business dashboard, push it further toward the brand.

## Component Conventions

| Element | Convention |
|---|---|
| Cards | `rounded-2xl bg-surface border border-[#27272A]` |
| Primary button | `bg-[#A855F7]` with white text, hover `bg-[#9333EA]` |
| Secondary button | Ghost with `border border-accent-highlight` or `border-purple-500` |
| Progress bars | 8px height, `rounded-full`, team color fill |
| Inputs | `bg-surface rounded-lg` with purple focus ring |
| Badges | Team color ring when earned, grayscale + lock when locked |
| Page bg | Radial gradient overlay: `radial-gradient(ellipse 80% 50% at 20% 10%, rgba(124,58,237,0.18) 0%, transparent 55%)` |
| Scrollbars | `.scrollbar-minimal` — 6px, zinc thumb, transparent track |
| Skeleton loaders | Used during all async data fetches (`SkeletonLine`, `SkeletonBlock` via PageShell) |

## Platform Notes

- **Framework:** Next.js 14 App Router · TypeScript · Tailwind CSS
- **Path alias:** `@/` maps to project root (no `src/` directory)
- **No `<form>` elements** — all forms use controlled inputs + onClick handlers
- **Coordinator-only UI** must be conditionally rendered, not CSS-hidden
- **Avatar system:** DiceBear bottts-neutral v9, customization stored in `users/{uid}.avatarOptions`
- **XP is global and cumulative** — every grant must log to `xpLog` subcollection
- **Team tier ≠ XP threshold** — tier advances only when all tier quests are completed
